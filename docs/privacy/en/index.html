Privacy Policy

Effective 17 October 2026 · Developer: Avraham Sason · App: Jew 365 / יהודי 365 (com.jew365.app)

The app runs entirely on your device. There is no account and no server of our own, and personal data reaches us only in a message you send. Nothing you enter and nothing the app computes is sent to us or to any third party, except in two cases described below: a technical crash report that carries no personal data, and a message you choose to send us from “Contact us”. The device backup your phone's operating system keeps in your Google or iCloud account, and the backup file you can export, are described under "Where data is stored".

What the app stores

Every item below is kept only in the app's local storage on your device:

  • Location — if you grant location permission, the coordinates are used to calculate halachic times. You can instead pick a city manually and never grant the permission.
  • Notification permission status — so the app knows whether reminders can be scheduled.
  • Preferences — nusach, language, theme, Israel/Diaspora, halachic opinions, which mitzvot are enabled, and their reminder settings.
  • Completion history — which mitzvot you marked done or skipped, and when.
  • Custom mitzvot you create.
  • Name and phone number — fully optional fields. You may skip them, and they are not validated. They are shown in the app's Settings and serve only as the starting values of the “Contact us” form, where you can change them. They leave the device only when you send a message.
  • Gender and marital status — see the next section.
  • Family purity tracking data, if you turn that feature on — see the section after it.

Profile answers: gender and marital status

When you start using the app, and in Settings, you can answer two questions: gender (male or female) and marital status (married or single). You can change the answers at any time in Settings. They are stored only on your device. They are used to tailor the wording of the interface and the default settings, and to decide whether to offer you family purity tracking. They are never sent anywhere, and resetting the app deletes them.

Family purity tracking

An optional feature. It is off by default and is offered only to users who state that they are married and have chosen a gender; a single user does not see it.

  • What is stored — the events you enter (onset, hefsek, bedikot, tevila, pauses and a rav's rulings) and your minhag settings. The count, the tasks and the reminders are calculated from them on the device.
  • Where — on your device only, in a separate encrypted database. The encryption key is held in the operating system's secure storage (Keychain on iOS, Android Keystore on Android) and is never included in a backup or a transfer to another device. On Android the data is excluded from Google backup. On iOS the device's iCloud backup may include the encrypted file, but never the key, so a copy restored from it cannot be read and the app deletes it. If the system does not let the app read the key, the data is kept in a separate file that is not encrypted. On Android that file is excluded from backup; on iOS it may be included in the device's iCloud backup. Family purity data is never included in the backup file the app exports.
  • Where it never goes — the data is not sent anywhere and is not part of crash reports.
  • Notifications — worded discreetly by default.
  • Deletion — in the tracking settings, "Delete the records" deletes the records only, and "Reset family purity tracking" deletes the records and the settings, replaces the encryption key and switches tracking off. Resetting the app deletes all of it, along with the encryption key.
  • Halacha — the app does not rule on halacha. In any doubt, ask a rav.

Biometric lock

The family purity screens are locked by default with your device's authentication: face recognition, fingerprint or the device passcode. The authentication is performed entirely by the operating system. The app receives only a "succeeded" or "failed" answer and never sees, stores or transmits biometric data. If the device has no biometrics set up, the lock does not apply. You can turn it off in the tracking settings.

Where data is stored

Data lives in the app's private storage on your device, protected by the operating system's app sandbox. We have no server and perform no backup or sync of our own. Two things keep a copy outside the app, and both are under your control:

  • Device backup — on Android, your preferences, enabled mitzvot, custom mitzvot, history and profile answers (including name and phone, if you filled them in) are part of Google's automatic backup to your Google account, and come back after a reinstall or on a new phone. On iOS, the app's storage is part of the device's iCloud backup when that is on. Google or Apple run the backup in your account, and we have no access to it. You can turn it off in your phone's backup settings. For family purity data, see the section above.
  • Backup file — under Settings → Backup and restore you can export a file with your preferences, enabled mitzvot, custom mitzvot, history and profile answers, including name and phone. The file is not encrypted. The app hands it only to the destination you choose in your phone's share sheet, and from then on it is under your control. Family purity data is never included in it.

What does leave the device

The app has no server of its own. Two technical components reach outward by themselves, and neither carries personal data:

  • Update checks (EAS Update) with Expo's servers — the app asks whether a newer JavaScript bundle exists when it opens, when you return to it, and in the hourly background refresh. The check transmits technical values only: app version, platform, and update identifier. It carries no personal data, no location, and no mitzvah history.
  • Crash reports to Sentry — see the next section.

A message from “Contact us” — under Settings → Contact us you can send us a message. Only when you tap “Send message” does the app send the name, phone number and message you filled in, together with the app version, the operating system and its version, and the interface language, to Formspree (Formspree, Inc., formspree.io), a form service that forwards the message to our email and keeps a copy of it in our account there. We use these details only to answer you. They are not used for marketing and are given to no one. To have a sent message deleted, email us at the address below. Formspree's privacy policy: formspree.io/legal/privacy-policy. You can also write to us straight from your own email app, without the form.

The halacha hotline link on the More screen opens your dialer or WhatsApp with the line's number, as you choose. The app sends nothing itself: the call or message is yours, through those apps.

The device backup is not sent by the app: your phone's operating system uploads it to your Google or iCloud account, as described under "Where data is stored".

Crash reports

In the release builds of the app (not in development builds), when the app crashes it sends a technical report to Sentry, a crash-monitoring service of Functional Software, Inc. (sentry.io), which processes the report on our behalf. Sentry is the only outside party that receives reports about what happens inside the app.

  • What is sent — the stack trace of the crash, the device model, the operating system version, the app version and the update identifier.
  • What is not sent — personal data, family purity data and screen content. IP addresses are not stored.
  • Control — the app has no switch to turn reporting off. Uninstalling the app stops it. Reports already sent are not linked to you, so resetting or uninstalling the app does not delete them.
  • Sentry's privacy policy: sentry.io/privacy

What the app does not do

  • No advertising and no ad networks.
  • No analytics and no behavioural tracking.
  • No sale or sharing of personal data with third parties. Formspree forwards a message you sent to us and uses it for nothing else.
  • No sign-up, no login, no cloud profile.

Permissions and why they are needed

  • Location (precise/approximate) — to compute sunrise, sunset, and the halachic times derived from them. Optional; choosing a city by hand is a full substitute.
  • Notifications — to show local reminders at the times you configured.
  • Exact alarms — halachic times are exact moments; without this permission Android may delay a reminder by tens of minutes.
  • Run at boot — so scheduled reminders survive a device restart.
  • Background refresh — a background task that runs about once an hour when the system allows it. It rebuilds the reminders for the coming days and checks whether a code update exists. The system may postpone it.
  • Biometrics / Face ID — to lock the family purity screens only, as described above.
  • Keep the screen awake (wake lock) — only while the siddur reader is open, so the screen does not turn off in the middle of reading.

Deleting your data

Two ways, both entirely under your control:

  • In the app: Settings → Device and notifications → App → Reset the app. This clears all preferences, history, custom mitzvot, profile answers, family purity tracking data and scheduled reminders.
  • Uninstall the app. Since no copy exists on any server of ours, uninstalling deletes everything on the device.

Resetting and uninstalling do not touch copies outside the app: delete the device backup in your Google or iCloud backup settings, and delete an exported backup file like any other file.

Children

The app is not directed to children under 13 and does not knowingly collect data from them.

Changes to this policy
