# AGENTS.md

## Purpose

- Own the main bottom-tab experience: more, history, home, siddur, settings, and tab layout.

## Ownership

- [_layout.tsx](_layout.tsx) owns tab registration and tab bar behavior.
- [more.tsx](more.tsx), [history.tsx](history.tsx), [home.tsx](home.tsx), [siddur.tsx](siddur.tsx), and [settings.tsx](settings.tsx) own their respective tab screens. The schedule is the stack route [../schedule.tsx](../schedule.tsx), opened from More and owned by [../AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md).
- [index.tsx](index.tsx) redirects hidden tab index traffic.

## Local Contracts

- Tab labels and normal UI copy must stay in [../../i18n/AGENTS.md](../../i18n/AGENTS.md).
- Every tab screen opens with [NavBar](../../components/NavBar.tsx), whose `actions` slot holds the screen's header controls (home's counter). Status strips are [Banner](../../components/Banner.tsx), section headings [SectionLabel](../../components/SectionLabel.tsx), pickers and confirmations [BottomSheet](../../components/BottomSheet.tsx) and [ConfirmDialog](../../components/ConfirmDialog.tsx), and a mitzvah's icon comes from `iconFor(mitzvah.icon)`: a tab never keeps a local copy of any of them, nor a text glyph in place of the icon tile.
- More, [more.tsx](more.tsx), is a hub of rows that open stack screens (the user's call, 2026-10-11): a [NavBar](../../components/NavBar.tsx) titled `more.title`, then one bordered card in the style of the siddur tab's catalog card ([siddur.tsx](siddur.tsx)) holding one [ListRow](../../components/ListRow.tsx) with `chevron` per destination. Its rows are the schedule (the `calendar` icon, `schedule.title` captioned `more.scheduleCaption`, a tap pushes `/schedule`) and, only while `hasChokContent()` holds, Chok LeYisrael: the `chok` icon, `chok.title`, captioned with this moment's portion (`chokPortionAt()`: `chok.parashaOf` with the week's names and the slot's `chok.slot.*`, or `chok.nothingToday`) on the `useNow` tick, and a tap opens `/siddur/chok_leyisrael`. Suite: [../__tests__/more.render.test.tsx](../__tests__/more.render.test.tsx). A new destination is one more `ListRow` in the same card; the hub holds no state of its own.
- The tab order in [_layout.tsx](_layout.tsx) is more, history, home, siddur, settings: home stays the initial route and sits in the middle of the bar, where [BottomTabs](../../components/BottomTabs.tsx) draws it as a raised gold disc (the user's choice, 2026-10-08). Keep it third when adding a tab.
- History should reuse pure helpers from [../../utils/AGENTS.md](../../utils/AGENTS.md) instead of duplicating day computation. Its today row and the streak start at the app day: `computeStats()` takes it from `now` and the location.
- History's [NavBar](../../components/NavBar.tsx) carries a `report.open` [HeaderPill](../../components/ScreenHeader.tsx) in `actions` that opens `/report`, the weekly report with its past weeks. Its per-mitzvah section lists only the enabled mitzvot that `isTrackedInRange()` ([historyStats.ts](../../utils/historyStats.ts)) holds for, those with a decided or still-open window on at least one day of the 30-day range, sorted by percent (the user's call, 2026-10-11): a mitzvah out of season, such as Sefirat HaOmer in Tishrei, never shows. While no mitzvah qualifies it shows `history.empty`, and not before the stats are computed.
- Day navigation should route to [../day/AGENTS.md](../day/AGENTS.md) for drilldown behavior.
- Home's day is the app day, `appDay(now, location)`, which turns at alot hashachar (the user's call, 2026-10-12: the night belongs to the evening before). Each card is the window `appDayWindow()` returns for its mitzvah, and every mark, skip, undo and open-text passes that window's own day, `dateKey(window.date)`, so after midnight a night window files under the previous date, the key its notifications carry; the completed list files a mitzvah with no window now under the app day. Between midnight and alot the evening's windows (maariv, the Omer, Chok LeYisrael, a custom window across midnight) stay current until they close, and one that closes before alot waits in "missed" until alot; next-up may be empty until alot; a custom window that starts after midnight is next-up the evening before, filed under its own date. The counter counts the mitzvot that have a window. The NavBar subtitle (date and parasha) follows the app day, while its Hebrew title turns at sunset. Suite: [../__tests__/home.render.test.tsx](../__tests__/home.render.test.tsx).
- Home shows a banner into `/checkin` while a check-in is open, and keeps the mitzvot waiting in it out of "missed".
- Home's header carries the date once: the NavBar title and subtitle. A missed card shows `home.passedAt` with the window's end and no ribbon, never the "expiring soon" state of a live card. The "completed" card lists skipped mitzvot too, labelled `state.skipped`, with the same undo as a completion, so a skip is visible and reversible. The long-press sheet is a [BottomSheet](../../components/BottomSheet.tsx) with two `SheetAction`s, details and skip; "edit" opened the same screen as details and is gone. Banner tones map to meaning: `warning` for missing zmanim, location or GPS and for the last-error banner, `accent` for the check-in and the profile prompt, `urgent` for denied notifications. `Banner` picks the text colour apart from the border colour, because the status colours read under 3:1 on their tinted backgrounds.
- The ready-update banner is not home's: [UpdateBanner](../../components/UpdateBanner.tsx), mounted by the root layout, floats over every screen and owns the foreground check (the user's call, 2026-10-10). Settings shows `appVersionLabel()` from [../../services/appUpdates.ts](../../services/appUpdates.ts). Home's foreground permission sync and scheduling refresh, and Settings' permission sync on focus, report a failure through `reportError('schedule', …)` instead of swallowing it, which is what raises the last-error banner.
- Settings opens with a bordered card (home's next-up card style) holding one [ListRow](../../components/ListRow.tsx): `settings.mitzvot` with `settings.mitzvotCaption`, the count of enabled mitzvot, and a tap opens `/mitzvot`. It is a plain row, never collapsible. Under it come three collapsible [SettingsSection](../../components/SettingsSection.tsx)s in this order: `settings.profile`, `settings.prayer`, `settings.device`. All three are closed on every visit (the `useFocusEffect` callback empties the screen-local `open` set; nothing is persisted), a tap on a header opens or closes that one, and several may be open at once. Closing Profile first commits the name and phone drafts, because a closed section unmounts its inputs before their blur fires. The `hilulotEnabled` and `roshChodeshEnabled` switches are not in Settings: they live on the library rows and on `/hilulot` and `/rosh-chodesh`. Profile holds the fields (name, phone, gender, marital status; the status labels follow the gender) and the taharah block. Prayer holds the nusach chips, the `settings.prayerMode` chips (`prayerMode.minyan` or `prayerMode.alone`, bound to `prayerMode` and `setPrayerMode`, the same field the reader's toolbar segmented control writes, with `settings.prayerModeHint` under them) and the siddur reading controls (auto-scroll switch and [ScrollSpeedStepper](../../components/ScrollSpeedStepper.tsx), 15 levels, writing the same `siddurAutoScroll` and `siddurScrollSpeed` the reader's toolbar controls write). Device holds four sub-headings, each a [SectionLabel](../../components/SectionLabel.tsx) in `goldText` over a hairline, so they read apart from the plain field labels: `settings.zmanim` (location name and status, "locate me", the city chips, whose selected chip is the saved location's city by `cityId` through `cityOf()`, and the Kriat Shema opinion chips), `settings.notifications` (the toggle, the `settings.weeklyReport` switch bound to `weeklyReportEnabled` with its hint, the OS status, the open-OS-settings button, the Android battery-optimisation row and the platform hint; the permission status refreshes through `useFocusEffect` whenever the tab gains focus, so there is no refresh button), `settings.display` (the [ThemeSwatchRow](../../components/ThemeSwatchRow.tsx) and the language chips) and `settings.app` (the `settings.logout` button and the version line).
- Settings subscribes through one `useShallow` slice of the user store, never `useUserStore()` whole, which re-rendered every section on each permission sync; the enabled-mitzvot count is its own selector on `useMitzvotStore` that returns a number. The reset button is worded as resetting the app: there is no account to log out of, and its confirmation is a destructive [ConfirmDialog](../../components/ConfirmDialog.tsx) that stays busy while `AppResetService.reset()` runs. The taharah block renders only while `taharahOffered()` holds and shows the tracking switch, the disclaimer and, while tracking is on, a button to `/taharah/settings`. The gender and status chips, the tracking switch and the nusach chips call [taharahOptIn.ts](../../stores/taharahOptIn.ts), the same functions the onboarding steps use. Field labels are [SectionLabel](../../components/SectionLabel.tsx)s, and a screen-local `SwitchRow` and `PrimaryButton` serve the repeated rows.
- The theme picker is [ThemeSwatchRow](../../components/ThemeSwatchRow.tsx): unlabelled colour circles for the six palettes, each writing `useUserStore.setTheme`. There is no system / light / dark mode switch. A change of gender sets the palette first (`chooseGender()` through `paletteForGender()`), and the user may pick any palette afterwards.
- Home's blocks run in one order: the [NavBar](../../components/NavBar.tsx), the last-error [Banner](../../components/Banner.tsx) when `useLastError()` holds a `schedule` or `reset` failure (`home.lastError`, `warning` tone, always above the priority banner; a tap clears the error and, for `schedule`, re-runs `NotificationScheduler.rebuild()`), at most one priority [Banner](../../components/Banner.tsx), `home.relevantNow` with the live cards or the empty state, one slim next-up [ListRow](../../components/ListRow.tsx) in a bordered card (`home.nextUp` with `home.nextAt`, the name and the window's opening time; a tap opens the details and a long press opens the same quick sheet as on a card), `home.missed` and `home.completed` (each section, heading included, is absent while its list is empty) and the taharah card last. The priority banner is the first that holds of missing zmanim (a tap opens Settings, where the city and location are chosen), denied notifications, an open check-in, a missing location, a GPS timeout and the profile prompt; the rest wait until it clears. The taharah card shows while `taharahEnabled` holds and no quiet block does: the cycle stage, then the first unfinished task of today, else the stage's hint. While `useTaharahStore.lockEnabled` holds it shows only the `taharah.home.title` heading and the `taharah.home.open` caption, because the session is always locked while home is showing. It opens `/taharah`. Its cycle, task and perisha data come from [../../utils/AGENTS.md](../../utils/AGENTS.md); home adds only the wording.
- Home offers a gold banner (`home.completeProfile`) into settings while onboarded and either the gender or the marital status is unanswered. It asks for the profile answers and never names the taharah topic, which a single user must not see. Pressing it writes the MMKV key `profile:intro-dismissed`, read once into state at mount, so it appears once; the key names the question set, so a new question gets a new key and every install is asked once more.
- Home's current and missed cards offer the open-text button only when `hasSiddurText()` holds for the card's window day, and open `/siddur/[id]` with `dateKey(window.date)`, the key the notifications already use.
- The mitzvot library is [../mitzvot.tsx](../mitzvot.tsx), a stack route opened from the settings row and the onboarding ready step; the siddur tab is [siddur.tsx](siddur.tsx), with the today group first.
- The siddur tab, [siddur.tsx](siddur.tsx), opens with a [NavBar](../../components/NavBar.tsx) (`siddur.catalog.title`, `siddur.catalog.subtitle` and the nusach) and lists `siddur.group.today` first: every `MITZVOT` entry that has a siddur text, each at its `currentOrNextWindow()`, as a [ListRow](../../components/ListRow.tsx) captioned `siddur.today.open` while that window is open, or `siddur.today.later` with the opening time when `hasSiddurText()` holds for a later window (`siddur.today.laterOn` with `common.tomorrow` or the weekday name before the time when that window opens on another day). An entry with no text today is not drawn in that card at all, rather than dimmed (the user's call, 2026-10-12), and when none qualifies the whole card, heading included, is absent. Rows sort by window start, a tap opens `/siddur/[id]` with the window's `dateKey` as `date`, and the `useNow` tick recomputes them. Then comes the `siddur.group.prayers` card: shacharit, mincha and maariv again, in that fixed order, taken from the same unfiltered entries and drawn by the same row function, so a prayer shows in both when it has a text today and, with none, only here, dimmed, not pressable and captioned `siddur.catalog.notToday`; nothing else is in it. After it comes every `STANDALONE_TEXTS` entry under its `SIDDUR_GROUPS` heading (meals; general, which holds Tikkun HaKlali, the bedtime Shema and the traveler's prayer first, then the blessings, the mezuzah and Kiddush Levana, in registry order, the user's call on 2026-10-12; festivals and celebrations) (a group with no text is not drawn), named in the UI language and drawn by the standalone row function with the group's icon. A text whose `available` condition fails for its `standaloneTextDay()` is dimmed, cannot be opened and shows its `availableLabel` (else `siddur.catalog.notToday`); the shared `useNow` tick re-checks it when a day turns. A `/siddur` deep link reaches the tab through the redirect in [../siddur/index.tsx](../siddur/index.tsx).

## Work Guidance

- Keep tab screens scannable and mobile-first; avoid turning operational app screens into marketing layouts.
- Preserve Hebrew-first and RTL ergonomics.
- If a tab consumes notification, location, completion, or settings state, use the owning store/service APIs instead of reaching into persistence directly.
