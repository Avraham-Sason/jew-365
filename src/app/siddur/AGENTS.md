# AGENTS.md

## Purpose

- Own the nusach reader route, opened from the notification "פתח נוסח" action, the mitzvah screen and the siddur tab, and the `/siddur` redirect.

## Ownership

- [index.tsx](index.tsx) is a `<Redirect>` to [the siddur tab](<../(tabs)/siddur.tsx>) and nothing else, so a `/siddur` deep link keeps working. The catalog lives in the tab, described in [../(tabs)/AGENTS.md](<../(tabs)/AGENTS.md>).
- [[id].tsx](%5Bid%5D.tsx) renders a text in the user's nusach and language. `id` is a text id: a mitzvah (static or custom, found through `findAnyMitzvah()`), else a standalone text (`standaloneTextId()`).

## Local Contracts

- For a mitzvah text, `date` is the civil date of the mitzvah window, the same `YYYY-MM-DD` the notification carries as `dateKey` (recovered from the notification identifier when the payload lacks it). Without a valid `date` (a bare deep link such as `/siddur/tefillin`) the reader uses today's date as the window date, for availability and for "סיימתי". The header subtitle always carries the Hebrew date and the occasions of the day the text resolved for, with or without a `date`. Evening texts resolve for the Hebrew day the evening opens, always through `liturgicalDay()` of that date; never derive a mitzvah text's Hebrew day from the clock directly.
- A standalone text needs no `date`: it resolves for `standaloneTextDay()` — the Hebrew day in effect now at the location, or for an evening text the night in effect or coming next — re-read on the shared `useNow` tick and memoized by day number so the text re-resolves only when the day turns. With a `date` it resolves through `liturgicalDay(date, evening)`, as a mitzvah text does.
- Availability comes from `hasSiddurText()` for a mitzvah text and `hasStandaloneText()` for a standalone one, and day resolution from `resolveSiddurText()`. The route never filters segments itself.
- "סיימתי" marks the mitzvah done for the window date through `markDone`, which also clears that mitzvah's notifications from the tray, with the same haptic as home's done button, and then lands on home whatever opened the reader: `dismissAll()` and `navigate('/(tabs)/home')`, or `replace` when the reader is the only screen (a cold-start notification tap). The user asked for this on 2026-10-08. A standalone text has no done button and touches no completion.
- The header is the shared [ScreenHeader](../../components/ScreenHeader.tsx), never a local copy, and carries only the back pill and the text name with the nusach, date and occasions line. Every reading control sits in one toolbar row, the header's body, directly above the text (the user's call, 2026-10-11), in this order: the section picker (with more than one picker entry), the auto-scroll play/pause switch (gold while on; the user asked for the switch in the reader on 2026-10-10), a [ScrollSpeedStepper](../../components/ScrollSpeedStepper.tsx) with `tone="header"` and `compact`, the A− / A+ text-size buttons (the user's call, 2026-10-08) and, when the resolved text has a `minyanOnly` section or a segment with a `minyan` label, the prayer-mode toggle. The toggle is a 32-point round switch like the play button (`accessibilityRole="switch"`, named `settings.prayerMode`, valued `prayerMode.<mode>`, checked while `minyan`), drawn as two person silhouettes in gold while the mode is `minyan` and as one on the pill background while `alone`; a press writes the other mode through `changePrayerMode()`. The row never wraps: the picker takes the leftover width, truncates its label to one line and keeps a 64-point minimum, every other control keeps its size, and everything fits on one line at 375 and 360 points in Hebrew and English. A control added later joins the row and must keep that true.
- Prayer mode is one persisted setting, `prayerMode` (`minyan` or `alone`), shared with the Settings tab: the toolbar toggle writes the same field. With a minyan the reader draws the text as it is. Praying alone, a `minyanOnly` section (Kaddish, Barchu, the Torah reading) draws as one closed fold in place of its heading and body: a toggle in the `minyan` color with the section title and the caption `siddur.minyanOnly`. Every labeled `minyan` block folds the same way, closed, under its label text. An optional block whose label has `alone: true` opens by default and the reader can close it. The folded section stays in the picker and `jumpTo` still lands on it, and the "נוסף היום" frames are unaffected.
- Which blocks fold and which open by default is decided by `aloneFolds()` in [siddur.ts](../../utils/siddur.ts), the one grouping the reader draws from (optional blocks, then the minyan blocks inside each); the route never groups segments itself. Fold state is screen state: `toggled` holds the keys the reader flipped away from their default, and a mode switch clears it so every default applies again. Keys: an optional section `${section.title.he}`, an optional block `${section.title.he}#${start}`, a minyan section `minyan:${section.title.he}`, a minyan block `minyan:${section.title.he}#${start}`, where `start` is the block's first segment index in the section.
- Auto-scroll is one persisted setting, `siddurAutoScroll` with `siddurScrollSpeed`, shared with the Settings tab: the reader's switch writes the same field, so pausing here also turns it off there. While it is on, the list scrolls on the UI thread (reanimated `useFrameCallback` on an `Animated.FlatList`), so the pace holds while the JS thread renders more sections. It moves only when the target crosses a physical pixel: through `scrollTo` on Android and the web, and through an animated `contentOffset` prop on iOS, because iOS Fabric reports every non-animated `scrollTo` as a momentum end, and RN's ScrollView then counts itself as animating for 16 ms and captures the next tap, which would make optional passages and "סיימתי" untappable while it runs. The pace is kept in lines: `SIDDUR_SCROLL_SPEEDS` gives lines per minute for each level and `autoScrollPixelsPerSecond()` turns that into pixels at the current text size.
- Auto-scroll waits a second after the text opens, the switch turns on or the reader comes back into focus. It stops while another screen is pushed over the reader (`useFocusEffect`), while a screen reader is on (its page scrolls would be cut short), while the section picker is open and while a picker jump is landing. It pauses during a drag or a fling and for 0.7 s after it, and for 0.7 s after any scroll it did not make itself: an offset more than 24 px away from where it put the list. On the web, which sends no drag or momentum events, a touch or a wheel event pauses it instead. Every resume starts from the list's real offset, so it never snaps back. It stops at the end of the text with the setting still on. Frames longer than 100 ms count as 100 ms, so a stall never jumps the text.
- Only a momentum end that follows a momentum begin counts as the end of a fling: iOS sends a bare momentum end after every programmatic scroll, and honoring it re-armed the 0.7 s pause on every step, so the text barely moved.
- The scroll handler (`useAnimatedScrollHandler`) also owns the drag events, because reanimated overrides a plain `onScrollBeginDrag` prop on the same list; the drag that cancels a pending picker jump reaches `cancelJump` through `runOnJS`.
- Keep-awake activation failures are swallowed: browsers refuse wake locks in background tabs, and Android refuses when the activity is gone.
- The source credits under the text are a CC-BY and CC-BY-SA license requirement, not decoration.

## Work Guidance

- Hebrew paragraphs are right-aligned RTL in the Noto Serif Hebrew family; English appears only in the English UI, under each paragraph. Native RN swaps a physical `textAlign` of `left`/`right` when `I18nManager.isRTL` is set, and react-native-web does not, so the reader aligns through `RIGHT_EDGE`/`LEFT_EDGE`, derived from `I18nManager.isRTL`. A literal `'right'` renders on the left of an Android phone in the Hebrew UI, even though the web preview looks right.
- A segment is highlighted as added today when its condition carries a flag (in `all` or `any`) that its section's other segments do not all share, or an omer day. Consecutive highlighted segments within one block share a single frame with a single "נוסף היום" caption.
- A passage only some say starts collapsed behind a tappable label with a down chevron, in the `optional` and `optionalBg` theme colors. An optional section's label replaces its title. An optional block inside a section sits where the passage is said, and the blocks come from `aloneFolds()`. Expanded text is drawn in `optional`. Expansion is screen state keyed as above. It survives `FlatList` virtualization and resets when the reader closes or the prayer mode changes.
- With a minyan, a passage only a minyan says stays open, drawn in the `minyan` theme color under a small heading that names who says it. Inside an optional block it takes the `minyan` color. Praying alone it folds behind a `minyan`-toned toggle that carries the same label, and opens in the same color.
- Both kinds of block carry a border on the side where the UI language starts. Like the text edges, the side is derived from `I18nManager.isRTL` and the language, because react-native-web does not mirror `borderStartWidth`. In the Hebrew UI, `borderStartWidth` landed on the left on the web.
- The body is an `Animated.FlatList` of section cards, so a long text such as Shacharit paints its first sections before the rest.
- With more than one picker entry, the toolbar shows one section picker. Its entries come from `pickerEntries()` in [siddur.ts](../../utils/siddur.ts): the text's chapters (Shacharit: the user's eleven stations plus Hallel and Musaf on their days, built by [the siddur build](../../../scripts/siddur/AGENTS.md)), or every section of a text without chapters. It names the entry the section at the top of the screen belongs to (`pickerEntryAt()`, tracked by `onViewableItemsChanged`), and opens the shared [BottomSheet](../../components/BottomSheet.tsx) titled `siddur.pickSection`, listing each entry as a `SheetAction` inside a `ScrollView` capped at 420 points, the current one passed `selected` (a `goldLight` row) and scrolled into view through the action's `onLayout` when the sheet opens. A pick jumps to the section that opens the chapter. The sheet gates itself on the quiet block and closes on a backdrop tap, its close button or Android back; the reader owns no `Modal` of its own.
- A pick jumps instantly with `scrollToIndex`. A section not yet measured is reached without moving the view: the list lifts its render window (`windowSize` Infinity) so the remaining sections render in the background, retries every 50 ms, and lands once the target is measured. It gives up after 5 s or when the reader drags, and then restores the normal window. Never scroll to an intermediate section while waiting. On a phone one section can be taller than the whole render window (Petach Eliyahu in Edot HaMizrach is about 12 screens), so the section after it never renders. A retry that snapped back to the last measured section therefore looped forever and locked scrolling.

## Verification

- Run `pnpm test -- src/app/__tests__/routes.test.ts` and `pnpm typecheck` after route changes.
- Check auto-scroll on the web preview at the mobile size with the pane painting (interleave small screenshots): it starts, pauses while the picker is open, lands on a picked section and continues from there, continues from a manually set offset, and stops at the end. A hidden pane throttles frames, so judge the pace by the unit tests, not the preview.
- Check prayer mode on the web preview at the mobile size: on a Torah-reading Monday, `/siddur/shacharit?date=<that Monday>`, press the toolbar toggle to יחיד and confirm the Kaddish, Barchu and Torah-reading sections and the Kedushah block fold, then switch back to במניין and confirm they draw open as before.
- Check visually on the web preview with dated URLs: an omer night, an erev Yom Tov, a Saturday night before Yom Tov, a fast-day mincha, a Saturday-night maariv, and Shacharit on Rosh Chodesh and Chol HaMoed; and `/siddur/birkat_hamazon` with no date; `/siddur` must land on the siddur tab.
