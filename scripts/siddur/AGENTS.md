# AGENTS.md

## Purpose

- Own the build that turns pinned Sefaria and Hebrew Wikisource siddur sources into the bundled, day-conditioned nusach texts the reader shows.

## Ownership

- [build.js](build.js) downloads each pinned source once into a gitignored cache folder next to it, parses segments into runs, applies the manifest, fills English, validates, and writes [../../assets/siddur/](../../assets/siddur) plus [../../src/data/siddurAssets.generated.ts](../../src/data/siddurAssets.generated.ts). It rewrites only files whose content changed, so Metro's watcher keeps working.
- [build.js](build.js) classifies the sources' small print line by line: a vocalized line is text to say, an unvocalized one is an instruction, and a leading unvocalized label ending in ":" splits off a vocalized line.
- [build.js](build.js) removes Sefaria footnotes (`<sup class="footnote-marker">` and `<i class="footnote">`) whole, counting nested tags of the same name (`withoutElements()`), because a footnote that italicises a word inside itself used to leak its tail into the English of hundreds of segments. `parseEnglish()` also drops a leading list number ("3. ") that some community translations print.
- [../../src/data/__tests__/siddurDump.test.ts](../../src/data/__tests__/siddurDump.test.ts) prints built texts as the reader resolves them on chosen days, with each day's flags, skipped unless `SIDDUR_DUMP` is set; its header gives the variables. It is the way to check a manifest change against the source day by day.
- A Wikisource source is fetched through the MediaWiki API at its pinned revision. Its `path` is a list of parts joined by ` | `; each part is a `{{#קטע}}` label, or `A .. B` for the text between label A and label B. A page with no labels is addressed by its headings instead: a part starting with `#` names a heading (vowels and spacing ignored, and it must match exactly one heading), `# A` is that heading's section up to the next heading of its level or higher, and `# A .. # B` is the text between two headings. Known templates become runs; an unknown template fails the build. The templates in a selected range are expanded and the rest of the page is left alone. `{{:Page}}` transcludes a whole page and, like `{{#קטע:Page|label}}`, only a page that is itself a pinned source, and every page a spec reads or transcludes reaches the credits. A wrapper listed in `TRANSPARENT_TEMPLATES` (`עם-ניקוד`) renders to its content before headings are located. A heading inside a selected range becomes an unvocalized instruction, as the sources' own instructions are, and `{{צת}}` (a quoted verse range) renders to nothing, because a psalm is taken from Sefaria.
- A Sefaria text that is a JaggedArray of depth 2 (Psalms: chapters, then verses) is addressed by its 0-based chapter index as the `path` (`'15'` is Psalm 16), and each verse is a segment.
- `flattenSmall` re-classifies a source that prints whole prayers in small print: it unwraps them and keeps only unvocalized fragments as instructions.
- `node scripts/siddur/build.js --inspect <sourceKey> "<path>"` prints a source leaf with its segment indices (`P`: small print promoted to said text, `N`: instruction only). Manifest indices come from this listing.
- [manifest.js](manifest.js) owns the source list (exact Sefaria version title or Wikisource revision, and credit) and, per text and nusach, the source section, range, groups, drops, edits, inserts, conditions, the passages only some say (`SOME_SAY` labels), the passages said only in a circumstance (`WHEN_SAID` labels), who says each passage only a minyan says (`SAID_BY` labels), and the per-nusach rule sets (`rule()`, `variant()`, `variantBefore()`) that turn the sources' inline alternatives into conditional runs.
- Two kinds of passage label exist, and a segment may carry both.
  - An optional label marks a passage the reader folds. `SOME_SAY` names a passage only some say. `WHEN_SAID` names a circumstance the date cannot decide: a house of mourning, an interruption between the hand and head tefillin, a fast day (open on public fasts), HaGomel, the bar mitzvah father's blessing, praying alone, women, the chazzan's Priestly Blessing when no kohanim go up (folded only in Israel for Ashkenaz), a meal begun before sunset on a day with an insert (below), a forgotten Yaaleh Veyavo, a guest at a meal, a sheva berachot meal, a brit milah meal, entering a city, travel by sea or air, the further Shema paragraphs at bedtime (Sefard), and the Chabad bedtime passage before marital relations. A spec's `optional` makes its whole section optional.
  - `WHEN_SAID.alone` ("המתפלל ביחיד אומר") is the one optional label that carries `alone: true`, which tells the reader to open that fold by default when the user prays alone. No other label may carry the flag.
  - `WHEN_SAID.maritalRelations` ("בלילה שיש בו תשמיש המטה") is the one label that carries `marriedOnly: true`, which tells the reader to hide the passage from a user whose marital status is single (the user's decision of 2026-10-12); a married user and one who has not answered keep the fold. Nothing else about the label changes, and no other label may carry the flag: [../../src/data/__tests__/siddur.test.ts](../../src/data/__tests__/siddur.test.ts) fails on one that does.
  - A minyan label (`SAID_BY`) names who says a passage only a minyan says: the chazzan, the chazzan with the congregation's answer, the repetition's Kedushah, Modim DeRabbanan and Priestly Blessing, the chazzan's Aneinu, mourners, the person called to the Torah, in the zimun the leader, the diners and one who did not eat, and `withMinyan` on each of the seven Sheva Berachot and on every 13 Middot verse. The reader shows it open under its label. A spec's `minyan` labels each segment of that spec, but never its authored `insert` notes.
- A long text is divided into chapters, the stops its section picker lists: `chapter(label, specs)` in [manifest.js](manifest.js) puts the label on the first spec of a section (`spec.chapter`, a `{ he, en }` label like a title), and [build.js](build.js) writes it on the built section and refuses a chapter on a spec that merges into an earlier same-titled section. The sections up to the next chapter belong to it. Shacharit is the one text with chapters, in the user's division of 2026-10-10: ברכות השחר (Modeh Ani onwards, where the morning blessings are contiguous: Edot HaMizrach and Chabad; Sefard and Ashkenaz open with מודה אני and put ברכות השחר at the blessings themselves, after יגדל), פתח אליהו (Edot), עטיפת טלית והנחת תפילין, מה טובו (Sefard, Ashkenaz, Chabad), קרבנות (from the Akedah, right after תפילת חנה in Edot), הודו (Edot, Sefard and Chabad, where Hodu precedes Baruch She'amar; the Hodu part of the Sefard source is split at its Baruch She'amar note, `to: 10` / `from: 11`), פסוקי דזמרה (at Baruch She'amar; Ashkenaz at its introductory psalm), קריאת שמע וברכותיה, תפילת העמידה, הלל and מוסף on their days, אשרי ובא לציון, בית יעקב (not Ashkenaz; Sefard only without Musaf, Chabad before Tachanun on a Musaf day), קוה אל ה׳ (not Ashkenaz), and for Ashkenaz עלינו לשבח and שיר של יום. Tachanun, the Torah reading, Aleinu and the rest stay inside the chapter before them. `T.hodu` exists for the split; [../../src/data/__tests__/siddur.test.ts](../../src/data/__tests__/siddur.test.ts) pins the chapter list of every nusach on weekdays, Rosh Chodesh, Chol HaMoed and Tisha B'Av.
- A whole section can be said only with a minyan. `MINYAN_ONLY_TITLES` in [manifest.js](manifest.js), built from `T` keys, lists the section titles (half Kaddish, Kaddish, full Kaddish, mourner's Kaddish, Kaddish DeRabbanan, Barchu, closing Barchu, the Torah reading, returning the Torah), and [build.js](build.js) writes `minyanOnly: true` on every section of every text and nusach that carries one of them, and on none else. The reader folds such a section for a user who prays alone. The flag survives `resolveSiddurText()`, which copies a section whole. Barchu and the Kaddish that sit inside a section with another title (the Shema blessings, Pesukei Dezimra, Korbanot) carry only their segment-level minyan label.
- Shacharit carries one section, `T.tallitAndTefillin`, built by `tallitAndTefillin()` in [manifest.js](manifest.js) from the `TALLIT_KATAN` and `TALLIT_GADOL` leaves and the same `TEFILLIN` leaves the standalone `tefillin` text uses, so the two never drift. Where each source prints it decides the place:
  - Ashkenaz (Metsudah Ashkenaz, Preparatory Prayers: Tallit, then Tefillin, after the Torah study) and Sefard (Metsudah Sefard, Upon Arising: Tallit, then Tefilin): after the Torah blessings and before Ma Tovu, which is where both sources put them relative to Ma Tovu. The Sefard source prints them in "Upon Arising", ahead of the washing leaf the assembled text takes right after Modeh Ani, and the section stays after the Torah blessings so washing still comes first.
  - Edot HaMizrach (Weekday Shacharit: Order of Talit, Order of Tefillin): after Petichat Eliyahu and before Hannah's Prayer, the source order.
  - Chabad (Shacharit: Tzitzit and Tallit, Tefillin): after the Torah blessings, ahead of the lulav and Ma Tovu, the source order.
  - The section opens, in Ashkenaz, Sefard and Chabad, with the tallit katan blessing, unfolded (the user's decision of 2026-10-12): the authored line `NOTES.tallitKatan` ("הלובש טלית קטן בלי טלית גדול מברך:" / "One who wears a tallit katan without a tallit gadol says:"), because the date cannot say who wears a tallit katan without a tallit gadol, then the blessing as regular text. `TALLIT_KATAN` in [manifest.js](manifest.js) holds those leaves for this section and for the standalone `tefillin` text, so the two never drift, and `tallitAndTefillin()` puts them first under the section's own condition, so Tisha B'Av skips the block with the rest. Edot HaMizrach prints no tallit katan, so its section has no such block. The tallit gadol part follows: its blessing with its verses. The tefillin part is the source's hand and head blessings with its instructions. Baruch Shem is printed by the Ashkenaz and Sefard sources only.
  - The whole section is skipped on Tisha B'Av (`none: ['tishaBav']`). The tefillin part also follows `keepsCholHamoed()` in [../../src/utils/skipRules.ts](../../src/utils/skipRules.ts): never on Chol HaMoed except Ashkenaz abroad. A condition cannot say "not (Chol HaMoed and Israel)", so Ashkenaz carries its tefillin twice, once for ordinary days and once for Chol HaMoed abroad together with the existing quiet-blessings note; every other nusach carries it once.
- The standalone `tefillin` text opens with the tallit katan blessing in Ashkenaz, Sefard and Chabad, unfolded after the same authored line `NOTES.tallitKatan` as Shacharit's section (the user's decision of 2026-10-12, replacing the earlier fold): `TALLIT_KATAN` in [manifest.js](manifest.js) takes the leaf the removed `tzitzit` text used (Ashkenaz's Tzitzit leaf, the blessing and the Yehi Ratzon after it; Sefard's Modeh Ani segments 5 and 6, starting after the source's own "before putting on the tallit katan" instruction at segment 4, which the line replaces; Chabad's segment 1 of Tzitzit and Tallit, the blessing alone) under the tefillin title, so it merges into the one tefillin section. Edot HaMizrach prints no tallit katan, so its text has none. The text ends with the three paragraphs of the Shema (`T.shema`, the user's decision of 2026-10-11) in a section of its own after the tefillin section and before Chabad's Rabbeinu Tam section, so the tefillin section still equals the one Shacharit prints: `TEFILLIN_SHEMA` in [manifest.js](manifest.js) takes the first part of the `SHEMA` leaves the standalone Shema of Shacharit (`krias_shma_shacharit`) is built from (Ashkenaz #1-#7 and Sefard #18-#24 with "El Melech Ne'eman" folded under `WHEN_SAID.alone`, Edot HaMizrach #10-#14 ending with the Emet the source prints there, Chabad #9-#13), leaving out the Emet that follows and the chazzan's repetition, so the text carries no minyan label and the reader shows no prayer-mode toggle for it. [../../src/data/__tests__/siddur.test.ts](../../src/data/__tests__/siddur.test.ts) pins both.
- The standalone texts are built like the mitzvah texts, each for all four nuschaot, with its builders near the end of [manifest.js](manifest.js):
  - Meals: `birkat_hamazon`, `al_hamichya`, `borei_nefashot`. General: `asher_yatzar` (the morning blessings' own leaves, restricted to Asher Yatzar), `birchot_hanehenin` (washing, the six food blessings, fragrance, Shehecheyanu), `birchot_hareiya` (lightning and thunder, rainbow, the great sea, trees in blossom, further blessings of praise), `mezuzah`, `tefilat_haderech`, `kriat_shema_al_hamita`, `kiddush_levana`, `tikkun_haklali`.   - Where a nusach's usual source has no leaf: Ashkenaz takes Me'ein Shalosh, Borei Nefashot, Tefilat HaDerech, the food blessings, the sights, the mezuzah and Chanukah from Daat Siddur Ashkenaz (`DAAT`, `daatName` for its bare "יי" and hyphenated names; Daat prints instructions as plain text, so an edit wraps each in `<small>`), its fragrance blessings and Sheva Berachot from the Metsudah Shabbat siddur (Sheva Berachot verified letter-identical to Hebrew Wikisource Ashkenaz, revision 3078438, which is not pinned), and its Kiddush Levana ending from its own Maariv Aleinu and mourners' Kaddish.   - The English of Ashkenaz Daat texts is the Sefaria Community Translation, used only where it aligns by index.
  - `tikkun_haklali` (the user's decision of 2026-10-11) is one text for all four nuschaot, in Hebrew only: `HEBREW_ONLY_TEXTS` in [manifest.js](manifest.js) keeps the translation memory from giving it English (Psalm 90:17 would otherwise take a Metsudah verse). It has twelve sections from three pinned sources: `T.tikkunBefore`, the ten psalms (16, 32, 41, 42, 59, 77, 90, 105, 137, 150, `TIKKUN_PSALMS`) and `T.tikkunAfter`. - Nothing said only on Shabbat or Yom Tov appears in a standalone text, because the app is never active inside a holy block (the user's Birkat HaMazon decision of 2026-10-08, applied to every standalone text); a passage skipped on those days carries `none: ['shabbat','yomTov']`. An evening text (`evening: true` in its registry entry) is conditioned on the Hebrew day whose night it is said on, so the bedtime Shema's Vidui follows `tachanunShacharit` of the next morning and the Chanukah first-night Shehecheyanu follows `chanukahFirstNight`.
- A meal begun before sunset keeps yesterday's insert. The day flags `roshChodeshYesterday`, `cholHamoedPesachYesterday`, `cholHamoedSukkotYesterday`, `chanukahYesterday` and `purimYesterday` hold on the day after, only when the day has no insert of that kind itself. Every Yaaleh Veyavo, Al HaNissim and Harachaman-of-the-day segment is conditioned on the day or its "yesterday" flag (`YAALEH_VEYAVO_OR_YESTERDAY` and friends) and folded under `WHEN_SAID.mealBeganYaalehVeyavo` / `mealBeganAlHanissim`, whose own `when` holds only on the day after, so the day itself shows the insert open.
- `optionalParts` and `minyanParts` take `[{ from, to, paragraph, until, label, englishInPart, englishOutside, en }]` and mark a run of segments inside a section. `paragraph` cuts the `from` segment where the run starts, and `until` cuts the `to` segment where it ends. The source English stays with the first piece unless `englishInPart`, which puts it on the labeled piece, or `englishOutside`, which puts it on the segment's first piece that no part labels (a segment every part labels keeps it on the first piece): the Sefard Shacharit sustenance prayer and the chatimah after it share a source segment, and the chatimah is the piece outside the fold that holds the English. A part's `en` is the exact run of the segment's source English that belongs to that piece; the rest of the segment's English stays where it went before (the `englishInPart` piece, else the first piece). A label may carry its own `when`, so the passage folds only on the days it is disputed. Several parts of one kind may share a segment when their paragraph ranges are disjoint (`paragraph` inclusive, `until` exclusive, a missing bound reaching the segment's start or end); `withLabels()` cuts the segment into one piece per range in paragraph order, and a paragraph no part covers stays a plain piece between them.
- A spec's `enMoves` carries `{ seg, text, to, before }` entries that move one exact run of source English from one segment of the leaf to another, before `enEdits`, for a source whose translation sits one segment off its Hebrew. `text` is the raw source wording, so an ampersand is `&amp;`; the run is appended to the target's English, or put in front of it with `before: true`; the build fails on a run that does not match exactly once. A segment whose English a move empties completely is marked like an `enDrop` segment.
- A spec's `enDrop` lists segment indices whose source English is not used, because it translates a different note than the Hebrew carries (a day list the Hebrew does not name, a commentary under an editorial note) or points at a print page: they ship Hebrew-only. The build marks such a segment `enDropped` while it runs and `fillTranslations()` skips it, because the translation memory is built from the same Metsudah Hebrew and English pairs and would otherwise put the dropped English straight back through the segment's own Hebrew; `main()` deletes the mark, so no asset carries it.
- An `enEdits` entry may carry `memory: true` when the English it corrects is not the source's but the translation memory's. `enEdits` run on the source English only, so a segment the memory fills (the source has none, or `enMoves` emptied it) could not otherwise be corrected or trimmed, and `enDrop` would throw all of the memory's English away. A memory edit is held until `fillTranslations()` has run, then applied by the same `applyEdits()` as every other edit (same `from` / `to` / `count` semantics, same exactly-once rule) to the English the memory placed on that segment. The build fails, naming the text, section and segment, when its `from` does not match as `count` says, when the segment received no memory English (it has source English, the text is in `HEBREW_ONLY_TEXTS`, or `enDrop` lists it), or when the segment is cut by a labeled part or sits in a `groups` range. Use it only for a segment whose English is the memory's by design; for source English use a plain `enEdits` entry, which the build checks against the source. Current case: the Edot HaMizrach maariv "ואומר החזן חצי קדיש" at the end of the Shema blessings, whose memory English is Ashkenaz's post-Yishtabach note; the edit keeps only its first sentence, because the rest says this Kaddish closes Pesukei Dezimrah.
- A source's `patches` in [manifest.js](manifest.js), `[{ from, to, count }]` on a Sefaria source, fix a typo the pinned translation carries once for the whole source: `loadSource()` replaces every occurrence of `from` in every string of the parsed text, in memory (the cache file is never written), so the segments and the translation memory read the fixed wording. `count` is the exact number of occurrences across the whole source.

## Local Contracts

- Both outputs are generated. Never hand-edit an asset or the generated map; change the manifest and run `pnpm siddur:build`.
- Every source pins an exact Sefaria version title or Wikisource revision id and carries a credit (title, license, URL). CC-BY and CC-BY-SA credits must reach the output; the reader displays them.
- The build fails, and must keep failing, on:
  - an edit that does not match exactly once;
  - a manifest rule that never matches, so a source update cannot silently drop a variant;
  - an edit, condition, group, `enMoves`, `enDrop` or `reviewed` index that targets a segment outside the selected range;
  - an omer section that does not carry exactly 49 days, each with its own date label;
  - an instruction note that names a day (`CONDITIONAL_INSTRUCTION`) on a segment with no condition and no `reviewed` entry;
  - such a note in the middle of said text without its own run condition, even inside a conditioned segment, because that is an unresolved inline alternative;
  - small print promoted to said text on an unconditioned segment, unless `reviewed` or matched by `ALWAYS_SAID`;
  - a passage label without Hebrew and English;
  - two optional parts, or two minyan parts, whose segment and paragraph ranges overlap, including two parts on one segment when either has no bound on it that keeps them apart;
  - a labeled part that matches no text or cuts past a segment's last paragraph;
  - an `enMoves` run, or a part's `en`, that does not match exactly once;
  - an `enEdits` entry on a segment that `enDrop` lists;
  - a source `patches` entry whose occurrences across the whole source differ from its `count`;
  - `patches` on a Wikisource source;
  - a part that carries both `en` and `englishInPart`, or both `englishInPart` and `englishOutside`;
  - an `enEdits` entry with `memory: true` whose `from` does not match as `count` says in the memory English, on a segment the translation memory did not fill, or on a segment cut by a labeled part or grouped;
  - both `minyan` and `minyanParts` on one spec;
  - a group that mixes labeled and unlabeled text;
  - a Wikisource heading that matches no heading or more than one, or a transcluded page that is not a pinned source;
  - merged sections that disagree on `optional`;
  - a `MINYAN_ONLY_TITLES` title that no built section carries.

- English is the aligned translation of the same source, else an exact normalized-Hebrew match from the Metsudah translation memory, else nothing. The English under a Hebrew segment must translate exactly that segment's passages: a sentence whose Hebrew sits in a neighbouring segment of the leaf moves there (`enMoves`), and a sentence with no Hebrew counterpart in the text at all (an editorial note, a variant the Hebrew does not carry, a dead print pointer, a leaked footnote tail) is cut (`enEdits`) or, when nothing of the English translates the Hebrew note, the segment's source English is dropped (`enDrop`).
